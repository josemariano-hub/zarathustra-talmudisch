[A2] The VNIR gate: bright AND spectrally flat
    Rule: mean(6 VNIR bands) > 0.55  AND  |slope6| < 0.08

    EPS / porexpan (clean)     PASS  <-- survives the gate 
    EPS / porexpan (soiled)    PASS  <-- survives the gate 
    PE silage bale wrap        PASS  <-- survives the gate 
    PE sheeting / mulch        PASS  <-- survives the gate 
    Limestone / gypsum         reject                      (bright 0.50)
    Sheep fleece (dirty)       reject                      (bright 0.44)
    Cereal stubble             reject                      (bright 0.28)
    Dry soil (Meseta)          reject                      (bright 0.20)
    Green crop                 reject                      (bright 0.17)

    -> Every NATURAL confuser is rejected. Only the white polymers survive,
       and porexpan is a white polymer, so VNIR cannot separate it from bale wrap.
       The gate removes the *numerous* confusers and leaves the *few*.

[A3] Why the deep-blue band matters
    Natural bright materials fall off toward the blue; polymers do not.
    material                    slope, 6-band  slope, 4-band  margin lost
    Limestone / gypsum                  0.247          0.160         35%
    Sheep fleece (dirty)                0.412          0.304         26%
    Cereal stubble                      0.579          0.475         18%
    -> Pleiades Neo's 400-450 nm band widens the separation from limestone by ~35%.
       This is a concrete reason to prefer it over any 4-band 30-50 cm sensor.

[A4] The SWIR features are diagnostic but unreachable from orbit
    C-H absorption at 1730 nm: 46% depth for porexpan,
    versus -14% for limestone. Unambiguous - if you can sample it.

    platform                           GSD  px on sphere   usable?  note
    WorldView-3 SWIR                 3.70m          0.03        no  regulator-resampled to 7.5 m for non-US users
    EnMAP / PRISMA hyperspectral    30.00m          0.00        no  full 400-2500 nm, 30 m
    EMIT (ISS)                      60.00m          0.00        no  free, but 60 m
    Airborne hyperspectral           1.00m          0.38       yes  light aircraft, full SWIR
    Drone SWIR / hyperspectral       0.05m           154       yes  at 120 m AGL

    -> The signature that would be decisive needs SWIR, and no orbital SWIR
       sensor resolves a 0.7 m object. It becomes usable only from an aircraft
       or a drone - which is the bridge to part B.

[A5] Effect of the VNIR spectral gate on the candidate list (50 km2)
    1 image                     5,573  ->      171 with spectral gate (97% reduction)
    2 images                    2,040  ->      101 with spectral gate (95% reduction)
    2 images + baseline           143  ->       27 with spectral gate (81% reduction)
    Residual is bales and sheeting - separated by size and shape, not colour.

[B1] At the 120 m legal ceiling, resolution is not the differentiator
     GSD is set by optics and the ceiling; every option resolves the sphere easily.

    platform                         GSD  px on sphere    swath         type
    DJI Mavic 3 Enterprise         3.2cm           22     170m   multirotor
    DJI Matrice 350 + P1           1.5cm           46     124m   multirotor
    WingtraOne GEN II RGB61        1.9cm           37     179m   VTOL fixed
    Quantum Trinity F90+           1.5cm           45     123m   VTOL fixed
    AgEagle eBee X (Aeria X)       2.5cm           28     152m   fixed wing

    Need only ~6 px across the sphere for confident ID. All RGB options
    clear that by a wide margin, so the binding parameter is SWATH x ENDURANCE,
    not camera quality. Buying a sharper camera buys nothing here.

[B4] The regulatory constraint dominates everything
     Coverage per pilot setup is capped by how far the aircraft may legally go.

    regime                            radius   km2/setup   setups for 120 km2  note
    Open category A3, VLOS              0.5km         0.8                  153  no paperwork; ~500 m visual range
    STS-02, BVLOS + observer            2.0km        12.6                   10  declaration, C6 aircraft, trained crew
    Specific cat., bespoke LUC          5.0km        78.5                    2  full authorisation, weeks of lead time

    -> Under plain VLOS a 120 km2 search needs ~153 separate pilot setups.
       That, not the airframe, is what makes or breaks a drone search.
       STS-02 is the enabling step: 10 setups instead of 153.

[B7] The pre-dawn thermal option
     Porexpan is a near-perfect insulator, so it has almost no thermal ballast.

    material                 thermal inertia   dT vs air, pre-dawn
    EPS / porexpan                    30 SI                -6.4 K
    Dry soil                         580 SI                -1.4 K
    Limestone                       1652 SI                -0.5 K
    Silage bale (wet)                917 SI                -0.9 K
    Cereal stubble                   120 SI                -4.0 K

    -> Porexpan sits about 5.0 K COLDER than surrounding soil before dawn.
       A 50 mK-NETD thermal camera sees that at ~100 sigma.
       At 120 m: 16 cm GSD, 4.4 px on target, 2.56 km2/h.
       Limitation: a 1-2 hour window before sunrise, and stubble also cools fast.
       Use it to confirm a shortlist or sweep a small high-probability core,
       not as the primary wide-area search.
