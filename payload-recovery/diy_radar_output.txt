[1] THE TARGET, AT OUR OWN FREQUENCY

    At 24 GHz (lambda 12.4 mm) the printed trihedral is enormous:
       leg a  RCS at 24 GHz   vs the sphere alone
         8cm          1.1 m2(porexpan itself: ~0.001 m2 - invisible)
        10cm          2.7 m2                                        
        15cm         13.7 m2                                        
        20cm         43.4 m2                                        

    Design point: 15 cm legs -> 13.7 m2. A truck-sized radar target folded
    from ~246 g of printed frame and aluminium sheet. The octahedral
    cluster keeps one corner facing any direction, so resting attitude
    does not matter. Shorter legs than the X-band satellite design work
    because OUR radar picks the wavelength - 24 GHz rewards small corners.

[2] DETECTION RANGE FROM THE SWEEPER

    Radar equation, noise-limited: R_max = 227 m  (13 dB SNR, single chirp)
    Clutter check at 200 m: cell 52 m2 x sigma0(-12 dB) = 3.3 m2 clutter
    -> signal-to-clutter 6 dB; a stable point over speckle, and
       coherent integration over ~100 chirps adds ~10 dB more margin.

    Operating swath ~340 m (side-looking both sides at 200 m):
    12 km2/h from the same 13 m/s glider - vs 4.8 for day RGB and 2.8
    for thermal. And it is indifferent to cloud, fog, rain, darkness and the
    sphere being under a hedge. Range resolution 0.75 m localises the hit
    to a strip a tractor can drive down.

[3] WHAT IT COSTS

      24 GHz FMCW eval module (IWR1443BOOST / K-MD2 class)        320E
      Raw-ADC capture board (DCA1000 class, used)                 220E
      Printed radome + side-look mount (X1C, PETG - no CF)          5E
      Cabling, power from existing 5 A UBEC stock                   5E
      TOTAL RADAR BAY                                             550E

      Plus the true cost: ~40-60 h of FMCW signal-processing work (chirp
      config, range-FFT, CFAR detection, geotagging on the Pi). That is DSP
      on a laptop, not microwave engineering - the module IS the radar.
      A from-scratch or harmonic-radar build (SDR + PA + LNA + two bands)
      runs ~800-1,000 EUR and 3-5x the hours; not worth it when a 5 EUR
      RECCO tab and a rented detector already give the clutter-free channel.

[4] THE NEXT-FLIGHT RECOVERY KIT, COMPLETE

      X1C-printed octahedral reflector cluster (Al-faced)      ~20 EUR, 246 g
      Iridium tracker on 4xAA L91 (the real fix)              ~300 EUR, 120 g
      RECCO reflector                                            ~5 EUR, 4 g
      Radar bay on the sweeper (above)                          550 EUR
      ------------------------------------------------------------------
      Three independent recovery channels for ~875 EUR total. The tracker
      tells you where it landed; the radar and RECCO find it if the tracker
      dies; the reflector needs no power at all and outlives everything.
      That is what "the next recovery becomes a non-event" costs.
