
========================================================================================================
  BATTERY LOGISTICS FOR CONTINUOUS SORTIES
========================================================================================================

[1] The formula, corrected

    Naive:   N = charge / flight

    Actual:  a pack is unavailable for (flight + cooldown + charge) after each
             launch, while the aircraft launches again every (flight + swap).

                 N_sets  = ceil( (flight + cool + charge) / (flight + swap) )
                 N_packs = N_sets x packs_per_flight

             and separately, charging throughput must keep up:

                 channels_needed = ceil( packs x charge / (flight + swap) )

    Cooldown is the term people forget. A DJI hub refuses to charge a pack above
    about 40 C, and in an August field in Castilla that is 15-20 minutes of doing
    nothing. It can add a whole battery to the requirement on its own.

    aircraft                         naive   +cool   sets   PACKS  ch. needed  have
    DJI Mavic 2 Enterprise Adv.        3.1     3.6      5       5           3     1  <-- need a 2nd charger
    Autel EVO II Dual 640T v3          2.6     3.1      4       4           3     1  <-- need a 2nd charger
    DJI Mavic 3 Thermal (M3T)          1.4     1.8      3       3           2     1  <-- need a 2nd charger
    DJI Matrice 30T                    1.6     2.1      3       6           3     2  <-- need a 2nd charger
    DJI Matrice 300 + H20T             1.2     1.6      3       6           3     2  <-- need a 2nd charger

    -> Three to four packs sustains a single-battery aircraft; six to eight a
       twin-pack one. Beyond that you are buying idle inventory, not airtime.

[2] What cooldown actually costs you
    Whether it tips you into another pack depends on how much headroom the
    aircraft's flight-to-charge ratio already has. Raw ratio shown alongside,
    so you can see how close each one sits to rounding up.

    condition                                M3T ratio   M3T packs  M2EA ratio  M2EA packs
    shaded, cool morning, forced-air fan          2.22           3        3.61           4
    spring / autumn, ambient 15 C                 2.43           3        3.91           4
    August afternoon, ambient 35 C                2.65           3        4.21           5
    midday in the sun, no shade                   2.87           3        4.52           5

    -> The M3T charges fast relative to its endurance, so it sits at 2.2-2.9
       and stays on 3 packs whatever the weather. The older M2EA charges slowly
       relative to a shorter flight, sits right on the boundary, and a hot
       August afternoon genuinely costs it a fifth pack.
       So: a 20 EUR fan and a shaded crate is worth having either way, but it
       only saves you money on the slow-charging aircraft.

[3] Field power - the constraint that actually bites
    To sustain continuous flight you must generate, on average, the energy
    the aircraft burns, divided by charger efficiency.

    aircraft                        Wh/sortie  avg draw  peak draw  field supply
    DJI Mavic 2 Enterprise Adv.          59Wh      127W       140W  car inverter or 500 Wh power station
    Autel EVO II Dual 640T v3            82Wh      152W       193W  car inverter or 500 Wh power station
    DJI Mavic 3 Thermal (M3T)            77Wh      118W       181W  car inverter or 500 Wh power station
    DJI Matrice 30T                     263Wh      442W       464W  1 kWh power station
    DJI Matrice 300 + H20T              548Wh      716W       967W  2 kW generator or 2+ kWh station

    -> A single-battery Mavic-class aircraft sips ~130 W average. You can run it
       all day off a car inverter. A twin-TB60 Matrice 300 needs ~700 W average,
       which is a real generator, fuel, and noise at 04:00 in a village field.
       For night thermal work that difference matters more than the spec sheet.

[4] Reality check: is the battery even the bottleneck?
    Under VLOS the pilot may only work a ~500 m bubble. Time to exhaust it:

    aircraft                          RGB rate   VLOS area  time to clear  vs flight
    DJI Mavic 2 Enterprise Adv.         4.15km2/h       0.79km2           11 min       39%
    Autel EVO II Dual 640T v3           4.15km2/h       0.79km2           11 min       33%
    DJI Mavic 3 Thermal (M3T)           4.73km2/h       0.79km2           10 min       24%
    DJI Matrice 30T                     5.09km2/h       0.79km2            9 min       24%
    DJI Matrice 300 + H20T              1.78km2/h       0.79km2           26 min       53%

    -> Every one of these clears its entire legal VLOS bubble in roughly a third
       to a half of one battery. You then have to pack up and drive to the next
       field. Under strict VLOS you are RELOCATION-limited, not energy-limited,
       and a fourth battery buys you nothing.

       Buying more batteries only pays off if you first buy more range:
         - an observer and an EVLOS arrangement, or
         - a second pilot leapfrogging setups in a second vehicle, or
         - a bespoke specific-category authorisation.
       Of those, the second pilot is the cheapest and doubles throughput outright.

[5] The night thermal sortie, which is the case that DOES need batteries
    Thermal sweeps a small core continuously from one setup - no relocation,
    because you are re-flying ground you already know rather than covering new.

    DJI Mavic 3 Thermal (M3T)
      10 sorties across a 7.4 h August night, 10 pack-charges
      3 packs cycling keeps it airborne all night (17 cm thermal GSD, 4.1 px on target)
      clears ~7.6 km2 of thermal core per night

    DJI Matrice 30T
      11 sorties across a 7.4 h August night, 21 pack-charges
      6 packs cycling keeps it airborne all night (12 cm thermal GSD, 5.8 px on target)
      clears ~5.3 km2 of thermal core per night

[6] Shopping list, and the one thing not to buy used
    Mavic 3 Thermal airframe + controller, used             4,500E
    3 x BS65 battery, NEW (see warning)                       810E
    2nd charging hub, so throughput is not the limit          220E
    2 kWh portable power station                            1,300E
    12 V fan + insulated crate for pack cooldown               60E
    Flashing green beacon for night operations                 90E
    Spare props, filters, SD cards, hard case                 400E
                                                        ---------
    total                                                   7,380E

    Pays back against contracting at 1,500 EUR/day in 5 search days.

    WARNING - buy the airframe used, buy the batteries NEW.
    Second-hand lithium packs are the one component where the saving is not worth
    it. You cannot see cycle count or storage abuse from a photograph, a tired pack
    sags under load and triggers a low-voltage landing over the wrong field, and a
    swollen cell is a fire in the back of the car. DJI packs are good for roughly
    200-400 cycles; a 10-day search at 10 sorties a day is ~25 cycles per pack, so
    new packs will outlast several campaigns. The airframe has no such wear item.
