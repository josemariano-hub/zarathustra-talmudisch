
========================================================================================================
  NIGHT SWEEPER - iterating the 1,940 EUR day sweeper into a quiet night aircraft
========================================================================================================

  Prior art check: this class EXISTS and works. Air Shepherd flew foam fixed
  wings with LWIR cameras, exclusively at night, over African reserves - 6,000+
  flight hours. We are re-implementing a proven concept at Meseta scale, not
  inventing one. The beacon stays on: quiet is a courtesy, conspicuity is law.

[ITERATION 0] Baseline: day sweeper as designed (Believer twin-boom, APS-C RGB)
    Night capability: NONE (RGB is blind at night)
    Noise at ground from 120 m: 31 dBA vs 32 dBA ambient  -> masked
    Cost: 1,940 EUR

[ITERATION 1] Bolt a cheap thermal core onto the Believer
    sensor                             GSD   swath px on target   detect   km2/h    EUR
    FLIR Lepton 3.5 (160x120)        103cm    165m          0.7      36s    5.55    250
    InfiRay P2-class (256x192)        45cm    115m          1.6     125s    3.88    340
    InfiRay Mini2-640, 9.1 mm         16cm    101m          4.4     125s    3.41    900
    FLIR Boson 640, 8.7 mm            17cm    106m          4.2     100s    3.57  2,950
    -> Detection is contrast-limited, so even the 250 EUR Lepton 'sees' the
       sphere - but at 1.0 m GSD every cooling straw patch is a candidate and
       nothing can be told apart. The P2-class core detects hard (125 sigma)
       at 1.6 px. The InfiRay 640 matches the Boson's geometry at 1/3 price.
       Noise and endurance unchanged - the airframe is still the limit.

[ITERATION 2] Swap airframe: twin-boom -> foam motor-glider (2.5 m class)
    AUW 2.0 kg, L/D 12.0, cruise 13.0 m/s -> 47 W electric (vs 82 W twin-boom)
    endurance on the same 150 Wh pack: 164 min  (sanity: foam motor-gliders publish 2.5-3.5 h - consistent)
    night air: rho 1.16 vs 1.1 kg/m3 (+5%), zero thermal turbulence,
    nocturnal boundary layer typically calm -> the quoted numbers are conservative at night
    14-inch folding prop at 2,800 rpm: tip speed 52 m/s (vs 72)
    noise at ground: 18 dBA vs 32 dBA ambient -> BELOW ambient: unnoticeable
    climb-glide option: glide 55 s (motor OFF - silent), climb 20 s
    -> 73% of the sortie is a gliding airframe with the prop folded;
       even the climb burst is 42 dBA at ground, a faint 20-second hum

[ITERATION 3] Final configuration: motor-glider + InfiRay 640 + dual payload bay
    thermal: 16 cm GSD, 101 m swath, 4.4 px on target, 125 sigma detection
    smear at 13.0 m/s: 0.8 px on a 4.4 px target - irrelevant (checked pattern)
    coverage: 2.77 km2/h x 164 min = 7.6 km2/sortie
    an August night (7.4 h usable): ~3 sorties, ~17 km2 of thermal sweep
    vs 7.3 km2/night for the DIY quad and 5.3 for the M3T - the wing wins on wing physics

    Bill of materials (day AND night capable):
      Foam motor-glider kit, 2.5 m class                          180E
      Low-Kv motor + 14-inch folding prop + spinner                95E
      Pixhawk-class autopilot + M10 GPS + airspeed                300E
      ELRS control link + 868 MHz telemetry                       110E
      InfiRay Mini2-640, 9.1 mm, USB board                        900E
      Raspberry Pi Zero 2 W logger + storage                       80E
      Swappable day bay: used 24 MP APS-C + 16 mm + trigger       530E
      Li-ion packs x3 (4S 10.4 Ah) + dual charger                 420E
      Flashing green night beacon + position strobes               90E
      Harness, printed payload bays, spares                       160E
      TOTAL                                                     2,865E
      (night-only, without the RGB day bay: 2,335 EUR)

  What the iteration bought, for +925 EUR over the day sweeper:
  - Night capability at Boson-class geometry (4.4 px, 125 sigma) for 1/3 the Boson price
  - 18 dBA at ground against a 32 dBA rural night - below ambient, the aircraft
    is inaudible in cruise and SILENT 73% of the time in climb-glide
  - 164-minute sorties: ~17 km2 of thermal per August night, 4x any quad option
  - The same airframe flies the APS-C by day: one aircraft, both jobs

  Chain to the mission: night thermal produces POSITIONS (detection at 125 sigma,
  identification at 4.4 px still impossible - established); the day bay or a car
  confirms them. Air Shepherd proved this exact loop for a decade; ours is smaller,
  slower, quieter and pointed at one styrofoam ball instead of poachers.

  Honest costs not in the BOM: ~50-60 build hours (the thermal USB capture
  pipeline is the real work), ArduPilot tuning flights, and the pilot's night
  rating admin. And the A3 rules bind at night exactly as by day: VLOS to the
  beacon, 150 m from uninvolved people, relocation between setups.
