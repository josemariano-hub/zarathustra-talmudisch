  Q = lambda * F/# / pixel_pitch      Q<1 detector-limited, Q~2 diffraction-limited

  camera              focal   GSD@120m   Airy FWHM      Q      limited by
  M3T thermal        8.35mm      17.2cm      10.3um   0.83  detector (pixel)    
  M4T thermal       11.87mm      12.1cm      10.3um   0.83  detector (pixel)    
  H30T thermal      23.74mm       6.1cm      10.3um   0.83  detector (pixel)    

  Pixel pitch is 12.0 um. The diffraction FWHM is 10.3 um,
  i.e. 0.86 of a pixel - the optics are slightly SHARPER than the
  detector samples. The null-to-null Airy diameter is 2.44*lambda*F = 24.4 um (~2 px),
  but resolution tracks the PSF core, not the first null. So the nominal GSD is honest.

  What the target actually subtends:

  camera                GSD  px across  px on target  with MTF loss
  M3T thermal        17.2cm        4.1          12.9            6.6
  M4T thermal        12.1cm        5.8          26.2           13.3
  H30T thermal        6.1cm       11.5         104.6           53.4

  DETECTION vs IDENTIFICATION - the distinction I glossed over
  ---------------------------------------------------------------
  M3T thermal       4.1 px across   detect   100 sigma   shape ID: NO - a cold blob only
  M4T thermal       5.8 px across   detect   100 sigma   shape ID: NO - a cold blob only
  H30T thermal     11.5 px across   detect   100 sigma   shape ID: yes

  Detection needs contrast, not pixels: a 5 K anomaly against a 50 mK detector is
  detectable at 2 px across. Identification needs ~6 px, and none of these deliver
  that on a 0.70 m target from 120 m. Thermal tells you WHERE to walk. It does not
  tell you the thing you found is your sphere rather than a patch of dry straw.
