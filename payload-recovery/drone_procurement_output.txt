  BUYING A DRONE FOR THIS JOB
========================================================================================================

[1] Does a fixed wing smear the thermal target?
    Uncooled microbolometers have a thermal time constant of ~8-12 ms.
    A moving platform drags the scene across the detector during that time.

    Target spans 5.8 thermal px at 12 cm GSD; raw margin is 100 sigma.

         speed    smear  smear px   peak kept  margin left  verdict
          6 m/s    0.06m      0.49        92%         92s  fine  (multirotor slow)
          8 m/s    0.08m      0.66        90%         90s  fine  (multirotor)
         12 m/s    0.12m      0.99        85%         85s  fine  (slow fixed wing)
         17 m/s    0.17m      1.40        80%         80s  fine  (fixed wing cruise)
         22 m/s    0.22m      1.81        76%         76s  fine  (fast fixed wing)

    -> Even at fast fixed-wing cruise the target keeps ~80% of its peak contrast
       and retains tens of sigma of margin. Smear is real but irrelevant here,
       because a 5 K anomaly against a 50 mK detector is an enormous signal.
       Fixed-wing thermal is viable; smear is not a reason to prefer a multirotor.

[2] How long is the usable night window?
    Correction to the earlier note: this is NOT a 1-2 hour pre-dawn slot.
    Porexpan reaches its steady cold offset within ~30 min of sunset, while soil
    keeps drawing heat from depth all night, so the contrast is open for hours.
    Waiting 3 h after sunset lets high-inertia objects shed their daytime heat,
    which is what makes the cold anomaly clean rather than merely present.

       month   night length   usable window  (sunset +3 h to sunrise)
         Aug         10.4 h           7.4 h
         Sep         11.8 h           8.8 h
         Oct         13.1 h          10.1 h
         Nov         14.3 h          11.3 h
         Dec         14.9 h          11.9 h

    -> August gives ~7.4 usable hours per night, not one or two.
       That is 3-4 fixed-wing sorties, or 6-8 multirotor batteries.

[4] The catch that decides which aircraft to buy
    STS-02 (BVLOS, 2 km from pilot) requires a C6-class aircraft.
    Multirotors carry C2. They are therefore VLOS-only unless you write your
    own SORA and obtain a bespoke authorisation.

    platform                              class                max regime  km2 per setup
    DJI Mavic 3 Thermal (M3T)                C2         VLOS only, ~500 m            0.8
    DJI Matrice 4T                           C2         VLOS only, ~500 m            0.8
    DJI Matrice 350 + H30T                   C2         VLOS only, ~500 m            0.8
    AgEagle eBee X + Duet T                  C6        STS-02 BVLOS, 2 km           12.6
    Quantum Trinity Pro (RGB+thermal)        C6        STS-02 BVLOS, 2 km           12.6
    WingtraOne GEN II (RGB only)             C6        STS-02 BVLOS, 2 km           12.6

    -> This, not coverage rate, is the real split. A 9,500 EUR Matrice 4T is
       an excellent instrument tethered to a 0.8 km2 bubble around the pilot.

[7] Payback across your own flight cadence
    Assume one balloon campaign needs a 2-day search when a payload is lost.

      losses per year     M4T (9.5k+1.5k)   Trinity Pro (48k+6k)
                    1              3.7 yr                18.0 yr
                    2              1.8 yr                 9.0 yr
                    4              0.9 yr                 4.5 yr
                    8              0.5 yr                 2.2 yr

    -> A ~10k EUR dual-sensor multirotor pays for itself in under four years
       at one loss per year, and in under one year at four. A 48k EUR fixed wing
       does not pay back on recovery alone - it needs other survey work to carry it.

========================================================================================================

RECOMMENDATION
  Buy the DJI Matrice 4T (or Mavic 3 Thermal if budget is tight). One aircraft,
  both sensors, ~10k EUR all-in, and it covers the thermal idea properly.
  Accept that it is VLOS-only: it is a precision instrument for a small area,
  a shortlist, or a whole-night thermal sweep of a high-probability core.

  Do NOT buy a 48k EUR fixed wing for payload recovery alone. If this ellipse
  turns out large, contract a Spanish STS-02 operator for the wide RGB sweep
  and use your own aircraft for the thermal follow-up. That splits the job
  along the line where each tool is actually better.
