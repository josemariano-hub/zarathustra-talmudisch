Editorial policy & methodology

Sources

  • German (Urtext) — Leipzig 1883/1891 first edition, retrieved from Project Gutenberg EBook #7205.
  • English — Thomas Common (1909), Public Domain, PG #1998. Other widely-cited translations (Kaufmann, Hollingdale, Parkes, Del Caro) are referenced in the inner-margin “Philology” gloss when they materially diverge on a key term, but only Common is reproduced verbatim in the centre column.
  • French — Henri Albert (1903), Public Domain, PG #5258. The first full French translation; revised editions exist (Goldschmidt, Reverdin) but are not in Public Domain.
  • Spanish — Andrés Sánchez Pascual, Así habló Zaratustra (Alianza Editorial, 1972), with editorial adaptation for re-typesetting; the translator's footnotes (numbered 1–579 in the source) are reproduced and re-targeted to verses by semantic match.

Paragraph alignment

Each chapter is split into verses (numbered paragraphs) using the German Urtext as the anchor. The corresponding paragraph in each translation is identified by LaBSE sentence embeddings + a dynamic-programming alignment that allows many-to-one (a translator merging two German paragraphs into one) and one-to-two transitions. Alignment quality is high in EN (Common, 1909, is itself a paragraph-by-paragraph translation), good in FR (Henri Albert kept the paragraphing close to Nietzsche's), and moderate in ES (Sánchez Pascual reorganized paragraphs more freely; semantic alignment is verified per verse by LaBSE cross-language embeddings rather than by surface position).

Commentary stack

Three regions following Talmudic typography:

Inner margin — close, word-level glosses: a hand-curated philology stream (Untergang, Übermensch, Adler & Schlange…); Sánchez Pascual's footnotes (the translator's own philological + biblical apparatus); short cross-references; and Diego Sánchez Meca's Spanish-language scholarly commentary.

Outer margin — chapter-level scholarly readings: Pierre Klossowski (Nietzsche et le cercle vicieux), Gilles Deleuze (Nietzsche et la philosophie), Laurence Lampert (Nietzsche's Teaching), Kathleen Higgins, Stanley Rosen, Robert Gooding-Williams, retrieved by semantic similarity to the verse.

Bottom band — context and background, the mesoreh of the Talmud page: the New Cambridge Companion to Nietzsche's Zarathustra; Sánchez Pascual's introductory essay; Loeb-style citations of the classical sources Nietzsche invokes.

Retrieval & quality control

For each commentary corpus we embed every paragraph with LaBSE and attach it to the verse whose German text best matches it (top-K=2 per chapter, threshold cosine similarity ≥ 0.50). A diversity cap prevents the same paragraph from being attached to more than three chapters. Then a per-side guaranteed-coverage pass attempts to keep every chapter equipped with at least one note inside, outside, and on the bottom band.

Every (verse, note) pair is then run through an editorial audit by Gemini 2.5 Flash, which scores the connection 0–3:

  • 0 — no plausible connection. Dropped.
  • 1 — weak or generic. Kept but rendered with a ? mark and reduced opacity.
  • 2–3 — reasonable to strong. Rendered normally.

Audit transparency. The prompt given to Gemini 2.5 Flash, verbatim:

“You are an editor checking the alignment of scholarly commentary to verses of Nietzsche's Also sprach Zarathustra. For each pair you decide: does the commentary plausibly illuminate this verse (not just the chapter or general theme)? Be conservative — a note that talks about the chapter's broader theme but does not engage what this specific verse says is at best a 1. Reserve 3 for notes that name a word/image/idea unique to this verse.”

A multi-language consensus pass then revisits each note's verse target by scoring the note against the verse in all four languages (DE / EN / FR / ES) and reassigning when the diagonal-weighted cross-lingual signal disagrees with the original DE-only retrieval.

Translation policy

Each commentary excerpt is written in one language only (Klossowski in French, Cambridge Companion in English, Sánchez Pascual in Spanish, etc.). For readers whose mother tongue differs from the excerpt's source language, the text is rendered in their mother tongue via machine translation (Gemini 2.5 Flash), with the excerpt's source language announced as a small flag badge next to the stream label (e.g. 🇫🇷 FR). The hand-curated philology stream is human-edited in all three languages. Streams shown without a flag badge (Lampert, Higgins, Rosen, Gooding-Williams, the New Cambridge Companion, Loeb) are source-English: no machine translation intervenes when reading ?lang=en.

This is a scholarly compromise: machine translation is fast and consistent but is not authoritative. When a wording is contested, check the original via the badge.

Caveats

  • Coverage is uneven. Of the 81 chapters, 13 currently lack an inner-margin (philology / cross-reference) entry, 6 lack an outer-margin (scholarly-commentary) entry, and 22 lack a bottom-band entry. These are gaps where no paragraph in the available corpora scored above the audit threshold. We expect coverage to grow as the corpora are expanded.
  • Translation coverage is also uneven. Of the 3 508 German verses, 555 currently lack at least one translation — 17 EN, 244 FR, 420 ES, and 13 DE (occasional verses present only in a non-German source). The alignment quality cited above is measured on aligned pairs and does not account for these absent rows.
  • Public-Domain English (Common, 1909) is archaic and at times stilted; the inner-margin philology stream flags places where Kaufmann or Del Caro give a materially different reading.
  • The Spanish edition has been re-typeset from a PDF and retains some OCR-era residues. The editorial pass strips footnote markers, page references, bullet characters, and notes containing clear scanner artefacts (spaced single letters, mangled apostrophes, mid-sentence fragments). A small number of ?-flagged notes may still read awkwardly.
  • Notes flagged with ? are kept because their corpus is otherwise relevant to the chapter, but the LLM audit was unsure they engage this specific verse. Treat them as starting points, not authorities.
  • The Sánchez Pascual stream re-uses the translator's original footnote numbers (1–579). When you see note №206, that is the footnote number in Alianza 1972, not a verse index of this edition; the surrounding chapter is the actual verse target.

Data export

The per-verse alignment table (3 520 rows, four languages) is available as a single JSON file: /source/alignment.json. Use it to build derivative tools, concordances, or your own reading interface. Released as-is, under the same fair-quotation terms as the rest of the site.
